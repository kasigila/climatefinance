Cultivating Change: Bridging Global Sustainability with Local Roots in Mbeya, Tanzania

At Africa Climate Finance, we believe that true sustainability is grown from the ground up. Recently, I had the privilege of stepping out of the boardroom and traveling to the fertile region of Mbeya, located in Tanzania, to visit a locally supported coffee farm operating under the Starbucks Farmer Support Centre's C.A.F.E. Practices—a true Shamba La Mfano (Model Farm).

This visit provided a hands-on opportunity to observe exactly how conglomerate-backed, organic initiatives are driving sustainable agriculture at the local level right here in Tanzania. Seeing the coffee plants thriving under these environmentally conscious practices reinforces the importance of ethical sourcing and ecological stewardship within our agricultural communities. It is one thing to discuss green initiatives in policy meetings; it is entirely another to see them taking root in the rich soil of Mbeya.

However, this visit wasn't solely a learning expedition; it was a powerful exchange of knowledge. I was able to share insights drawn from my own decade-long journey through climate finance, backed by over 25 years of experience in the banking sector. Discussing sustainable funding models and green policies with those on the frontlines of agriculture helps bridge the critical gap between high-level financial strategy and grassroots implementation.

As the Group CEO and Founder of Africa Climate Finance, these are the exact models of resilience and sustainability we aim to champion, finance, and scale. The future of our continent's agriculture depends on innovative climate financing that directly empowers the farmers who feed our communities and protect our ecosystems.
