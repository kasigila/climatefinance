COP30 Brazil — Belém 2025

COP30 Scenes in Brazil: A Financial Mandate for the Green Decade

This global summit confirmed a profound shift, establishing explicit, massive financial targets that define the next decade of green investment and create an unprecedented mandate for the financial sector. CRDB Bank Plc was well represented, showcasing the commitment of leading African financial institutions to the global climate agenda.

The key outcomes include:

  1. The Baku-to-Belém Roadmap: This roadmap aims to unlock at least $1.3 Trillion annually in climate finance for developing nations by 2035.
  2. A new political signal to at least triple adaptation finance in the same period—a tripling that represents an estimated scale of investment nearing $120 billion annually for resilience measures.

These figures underscore the transition of climate action from a passive risk concern to a primary investment strategy for the financial sector, offering both systematic risk mitigation and major, high-growth opportunities across global markets.

The Dialogue on Climate Adaptation in Africa at COP30

Transformative approaches for adaptation planning in Africa: Experiences and Lessons

I had the immense privilege of representing CRDB Bank Plc on the global stage at COP30 in Belém, Brazil, participating in the high-level panel discussion: "Transformative approaches for adaptation planning in Africa: Experiences and Lessons."

Hosted by the Global Center on Adaptation (GCA) and CGIAR at the Food & Agriculture Pavilion (PV-E170, Blue Zone) on Tuesday, 11th November 2025, this session was a vital platform. We focused on demonstrating how the African private sector is actively embedding climate resilience into its core strategy. The goal is clear: to move beyond planning and accelerate tangible investments that safeguard food security and protect livelihoods across the continent.

Let's continue to collaborate and build resilient, climate-smart African food systems together.

On Green Taxonomy and Tanzania

My participation in the COP30 discussion was highly insightful, focusing on the operational difficulties of creating green products when a national 'taxonomy' is missing. This requires issuers to undertake the complex and resource-intensive task of building an unwritten, self-imposed green taxonomy by blending various international market principles. This fragmented approach clearly underscores the critical need for Tanzania to transition to a harmonized national standard. I am proud that Tanzania is already on track to develop its own taxonomy, a vital step toward unlocking green capital efficiently.

International Engagement

COP29 Azerbaijan — Baku 2024

At COP29, participating in side events hosted by the COP29 Presidency at the Tanzania Pavilion in Baku, Azerbaijan, specifically on Finance Day. This event took place as part of the Ministerial Meeting agenda of the Coalition of Finance Ministers for Climate Action, which was held on November 14, 2024. Our focus was on the best practices and challenges associated with allocating and mobilizing financing for the implementation of Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs).

I had the opportunity to share the achievements of CRDB Bank, including our collaboration with MUFG Bank, as detailed in the Green Climate Fund projects Project FP223 (total project value US$1.5b) and Project FP179 (total project value US$200m). Additionally, I highlighted many other aspects of CRDB Bank's impressive ESG journey that we have pursued alongside my colleagues and various stakeholders.

The discussions aimed to identify effective practices for coordinating diverse stakeholders and resources while ensuring alignment with strategic climate and development priorities. The insights gained are expected to inform the COP29 Action Agenda and support ongoing discussions as we move toward COP30.
