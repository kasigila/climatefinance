Climate Change Challenge

Climate change is already happening: temperatures are rising, drought and wild fires are starting to occur more frequently, rainfall patterns are shifting, glaciers and snow are melting and the global mean sea level is rising.

The latest Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) report shows greenhouse gas emissions continue to rise, and current plans to address climate change are not ambitious enough to limit warming to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels—a threshold scientists believe is necessary to avoid even more catastrophic impacts.

Africa has contributed the least to the global accumulation of greenhouse gas emissions: less than 4% of global CO2 emissions come from the African continent. However, this region is more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change than any other. Africa is already highly susceptible to droughts, which are linked to decreases in agricultural yields and in turn, increases in food prices.

Our Mission

We fill the gap of Climate Finance in Africa via MicroFinance for the private and vulnerable communities.

Our overarching goals align with low-carbon, climate-resilient pathways and adaptation outcomes. We focus on three pillars: Keep 1.5°C Alive, Mobilize Support for Climate Solutions, and Foster Partnerships.

Our Pathway

We establish the flagship Climate-Smart Microfinance Initiative in Tanzania, using the new Central Bank of Tanzania license to de-risk and scale climate investment. From Tanzania, we replicate and scale proven models across Africa.

Our Impact

Women Economic Empowerment

Taking Climate Finance to the Grassroots: Empowering 280+ Women in Rungwe, Tanzania 🇹🇿🌱

During my annual leave earlier this January 2026, I returned to the village to sit down with my community in Rungwe, Tanzania. I had the privilege of engaging with the true backbone of our society who are the mothers, smallholder farmers and neighbors of Isajilo Ward.

We did not just have a class because we started a movement. I managed to reach more than 280 women who are in critical need of financial literacy, climate change knowledge and microfinance support.

My mission was to translate the complex language of mainstream banking and the harsh paradox of climate change into the language of my people. We stripped away the jargon to discuss real issues affecting their harvests. This inspired me to design a tailored and grassroots friendly introductory course in a questionnaire format. The goal is to guide them on adopting Climate Smart Technologies and Sustainable Practices. We are also looking at how to connect them with available microfinance solutions to capacitate them to navigate these climate shocks. This initiative is effectively supported by the strong regulatory framework and the Microfinance Act which creates an enabling environment for these financial solutions.
